How Modes Are Assigned

Each ETF's weekly price is divided by the benchmark to get its relative strength. Two normalised readings are derived, both centred on 100:

  • RS-Ratio: is the ETF outperforming (>100) or underperforming (<100) the benchmark versus its own 10-week trend?
  • RS-Momentum: is that relative strength improving (>100) or fading (<100)?
◤ RecoveringRS-Ratio < 100 · RS-Momentum ≥ 100
▲ AcceleratingRS-Ratio ≥ 100 · RS-Momentum ≥ 100
▼ UnderperformingRS-Ratio < 100 · RS-Momentum < 100
◢ DeceleratingRS-Ratio ≥ 100 · RS-Momentum < 100

ETFs typically rotate clockwise: Recovering → Accelerating → Decelerating → Underperforming. Relative moves smaller than 1% are treated as noise, so ETFs that track the benchmark sit near the centre with low confidence.

Questions
How current is the data?

Prices are end-of-day NSE closes, refreshed after market close every trading day. The latest week includes trading up to the date shown at the top of the page. Newly listed ETFs are added automatically once they have enough trading history.

What does "Confidence" mean?

It cross-checks the mode against simple relative returns: whether the ETF has actually beaten the benchmark over 13 weeks, whether its recent relative return is improving, and whether it trades actively. High means all checks agree.

Why is an ETF "Not classified"?

At least 20 weeks of clean trading history are needed. Recently listed, suspended or rarely traded ETFs are listed but not classified, to avoid misleading signals.

Which benchmark should I use?

Nifty 50 suits most equity ETFs. Compare sector ETFs with their parent index (e.g. Bank ETFs vs Nifty Bank) to see whether they lead within their sector, and gold or silver ETFs vs Gold.
