Wearable

The term "wearable" means "something that can be worn," and is used in phrases like "wearable device." Wearable devices

A foundation is being built that allows people to easily access various money-related services from their smartphones and computers. Nanotechnology

Nanotechnology is the technology of controlling matter at the atomic and molecular level.
The concept itself was born in 1959, but true "nanotechnology" is considered to have begun in 1981 with the development of microscopes that could "see" atoms.
In recent years, it is expected to bring development not only to the electronics industry, such as semiconductors, but also to biotechnology products that perform complex tasks within the human body. In
2023, three people from a US university and company were awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for discovering quantum dots, which are nano-sized semiconductor crystals, and developing methods for manufacturing them. Applications in displays and medical fields are expected, and it will continue to attract attention.

Virtual reality

Virtual reality (VR) is a technology that simulates user experiences in a virtual space.
Since 2016, which was called the "Year of VR," product development has progressed, and various types of VR headsets are now sold at consumer electronics stores, making it possible for ordinary households to enjoy VR for games and other purposes. In addition, there are augmented reality (AR), which adds new visuals and sounds to the real environment, and mixed reality (MR), which allows users to approach and touch virtual objects projected into the real world, and the use of virtual spaces is expanding according to the application.
Future applications are expected in many fields, such as immersive video games, holographic communication, and virtual surgery platforms.

drone

A drone is an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) or unmanned aircraft (UAS).
Initially popularized for military use, drones are now gaining attention for their use in entertainment and commerce. With the emergence of a variety of non-military drones with increased payload capacity, they are expected to be used in many fields, including aerial videography, equipment inspection in difficult locations, agriculture, logistics, and racing, and some are already in practical use.
In 2020, the U.S. Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) announced new regulations in anticipation of the expansion of commercial drone use, requiring aircraft registration and the transmission of ID and controller location information. As legal frameworks for drone use progress, a future where drones play an integral role in our daily lives may not be so far away.

Genetic engineering

Genetic engineering is a branch of biotechnology that modifies the genes of humans, animals, plants, and other organisms to treat diseases and improve crop varieties.
In the United States, more than 90% of the cultivated land for soybeans and corn is already genetically modified*.
Since the emergence of CRISPR-Cas9 in 2012, a new technology that allows for the easy and rapid modification of genes in living organisms, genetic engineering has entered a new stage.
Further developments in gene therapy using human genome editing are expected.
Genetic engineering, which contributes to groundbreaking medical treatments and drugs, as well as the production of highly nutritious and resilient crops and livestock, is a cutting-edge field that is shaping our future from the microscopic world.

Hydrogen economy

The hydrogen economy refers to all economic activities that utilize hydrogen as energy and fuel, including the production, storage, and manufacturing of products related to hydrogen transportation.
In order to realize a decarbonized society, the use of hydrogen energy has been identified as a policy priority in many countries and regions, including the United States and Europe. In November 2020, the U.S. Department of Energy published the "Hydrogen Program Plan" for hydrogen research and development, indicating a direction toward the nationwide utilization of hydrogen.
Against this backdrop, the market size of the hydrogen economy in the United States is projected to reach US$640 billion by 2030 and US$1,410 billion by 2050*.

*Source: Compiled by Mitsubishi UFJ Asset Management based on data from Statista and the U.S. Department of Energy.
