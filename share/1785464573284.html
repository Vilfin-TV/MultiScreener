Your Complete Guide to Yokohama Hakkeijima Sea Paradise — Every Attraction, Every Pass, Every Discount, and the Smartest Way to Get There from Tokyo

July 31, 2026

▶

Press play to ride the massive roller coaster at Yokohama Hakkeijima Sea Paradise — experience the thrill before you plan your trip!

There are places in Japan that defy easy categorisation. Not quite an aquarium. Not quite a theme park. Not quite a resort. Yokohama Hakkeijima Sea Paradise sits in its own category entirely — a self-contained world on a man-made island in Yokohama Bay where the ocean meets adrenaline, where beluga whales share a postcode with roller coasters, where a family can move in a single afternoon from the silence of a deep-water tank to the speed of a coastal drop ride at 100 kilometres per hour. This is your complete guide to one of Japan's most extraordinary days out — every attraction, every ticket option, every discount, and the insider knowledge that transforms a good visit into an unforgettable one.

What Is Hakkeijima Sea Paradise — And Why Is It Different?

Yokohama Hakkeijima Sea Paradise opened on March 18, 1993, built entirely on reclaimed land in Kanazawa Ward, southern Yokohama. The name means Island of Eight Views, and the island — connected to the mainland by a bridge — was purpose-built for the facility. Unlike most Japanese amusement parks, which are land-locked urban attractions, Hakkeijima is surrounded on three sides by actual ocean. The sea is not a backdrop. It is the point.

The facility is divided into four distinct zones, each requiring separate thought before you visit: Aqua Resorts (the aquarium complex), Pleasure Land (the rides and amusement attractions), Sea Farm (hands-on fishing and marine experiences), and Fureai Lagoon (touch-pool and interactive marine zones). You do not need to visit all four. Most visitors focus on two or three. Knowing the zones before you arrive is the single biggest factor in making your day efficient.

All Attractions — Zone by Zone

Zone 1 — Aqua Resorts (The Aquarium Complex)

Aqua Resorts is the heart of Hakkeijima and the reason most first-time visitors come. It comprises four interconnected aquarium buildings housing over 70,000 marine creatures across hundreds of species.

  • Aqua Theater — the main performance arena, seating thousands, hosting the flagship dolphin and sea lion shows. Multiple performances daily; schedule posted at the entrance and on the official app. Arrive 20 minutes early for front-section seating.
  • Beluga Whale Tank — one of Japan's most popular marine exhibits, a deep-water tank with floor-to-ceiling viewing windows where beluga whales swim at eye level. The belugas are named and individually known to staff; the interaction programmes allow close-range viewing sessions.
  • Shark Tank & Deep Ocean Zone — multi-floor open-ocean tanks featuring sharks, rays, and large pelagic fish. The overhead tunnel section (walking under a tank of sharks) is a highlight for adults and children alike.
  • Jellyfish Zone — a dedicated gallery of illuminated jellyfish tanks, covering over 50 species. Best visited in the late afternoon when natural light through the windows interacts with the tank lighting.
  • Penguin & Seal Zone — enclosed arctic environment with Humboldt and Gentoo penguins, harbour seals, and a feeding channel that allows visitors to track animals as they swim past at speed.
  • Walrus Exhibit — one of only a handful of aquariums in Japan housing walruses. Performance sessions feature trained behaviours demonstrating walrus intelligence, strength, and agility.
  • Aqua Live (Night Aquarium) — on selected evenings, the aquarium complex transforms with special lighting, projected visuals, and ambient music. Tickets sold separately and in advance. Extremely popular — book weeks ahead in summer.

Zone 2 — Pleasure Land (Rides & Amusement)

Pleasure Land runs along the ocean-facing side of the island and contains twelve rides ranging from family-friendly carousels to serious thrill machines. The defining feature of Pleasure Land is that many rides travel over or near the actual ocean — the coastline integration is unlike anything at inland amusement parks.

  • Blue Fall — the signature ride. A 107-metre free-fall tower drop, currently one of the tallest drop rides in Japan. The view from the top extends across Yokohama Bay to Tokyo Bay on clear days. The drop itself takes approximately three seconds. Three seconds that most people describe in ways that cannot be printed here.
  • Sea Breeze — a coastal roller coaster that runs directly over the water's edge, banking sharply over the bay. Maximum speed: approximately 75 km/h. The water below is real, the air is salt-scented, and the combination produces an experience that purely inland coasters cannot replicate.
  • Surf Coaster Leviathan — the massive roller coaster featured in the YouTube video. A full-circuit steel coaster with multiple inversions, reaching speeds of around 100 km/h, with sections that overhang the ocean. The Leviathan is ranked among the top coastal coasters in Asia by specialist enthusiast communities. Height restriction: 120 cm.
  • Aqua Ride — a log flume water ride, moderate speed, appropriate for families with children over 90 cm. Budget for being significantly wet.
  • Marine Bicycle (Sky Cycle) — a pedal-powered suspended cycle ride along an elevated track with ocean views. Slow, family-friendly, genuinely beautiful on clear days.
  • Dolphin Fantasy — a gentler dark ride through an illuminated underwater fantasy environment. Suitable for all ages including very young children.
  • Additional rides: carousel, spinning teacup-style ride, tower swing (Jet Coaster), and seasonal special installations in summer and winter.

Zone 3 — Sea Farm

Sea Farm is Hakkeijima's least-discussed and most underrated zone. It is a working marine aquaculture experience: visitors can fish for their own seafood using provided equipment in a managed sea-fishing pond, then have their catch cooked on-site and served as a meal. The zone also features seasonal shellfish gathering, crab catching, and flounder fishing pools. Sea Farm operates on a pay-per-catch basis for some activities — budget approximately ¥1,000–¥2,000 per person depending on activity. It opens and closes based on tidal and seasonal conditions; check the website the morning of your visit.

Zone 4 — Fureai Lagoon (Touch & Interact)

Fureai means "touching/meeting" in Japanese. This zone is dedicated to hands-on marine interaction: touch pools for starfish, horseshoe crabs, and small sharks; a wading pool where visitors can interact with dolphins in shallow water (the Dolphin Touch Programme, booked separately and capacity-limited); and the Seal Interaction Programme, where visitors can hand-feed harbour seals in a guided session. Fureai Lagoon is essential for families with children under 10 and for any visitor who wants more than passive observation.

How to Travel from Tokyo

Hakkeijima is approximately 50–65 minutes from central Tokyo by public transport. There are three practical routes, each with different trade-offs.

Route 1 — Keikyu Line (Recommended)

This is the fastest and most direct connection and comes with a significant built-in discount advantage (see below). From Shinagawa Station, take the Keikyu Main Line Limited Express toward Kanazawa Hakkei or Uraga. Ride to Kanazawa Hakkei Station (approximately 35–40 minutes from Shinagawa). From Kanazawa Hakkei, transfer to the Kanazawa Seaside Line — a fully automated monorail-style line — and ride three stops to Hakkeijima Station. Total travel time from Shinagawa: approximately 45–50 minutes. From Tokyo Station, add 10–15 minutes via JR to Shinagawa.

Route 2 — JR Yokohama + Kanazawa Seaside Line

Take the JR Keihin-Tohoku Line to Shin-Sugita Station or Isogo Station, then transfer to the Kanazawa Seaside Line to Hakkeijima Station (approximately 15–20 minutes on the Seaside Line). Total from Yokohama Station: approximately 45 minutes. From Tokyo: 60–70 minutes depending on starting point.

Route 3 — Direct Bus (Seasonal)

During peak season (July–August, Golden Week, winter holidays), direct tourist buses operate from Yokohama Station (East Exit) and from select Tokyo departure points. The bus drops directly at the Hakkeijima entrance. Approximately 40–50 minutes from Yokohama Station; 80–90 minutes from central Tokyo. Check the Hakkeijima website for seasonal bus schedules — they vary year to year.

By Car

From the Wangan Expressway: exit at Kanazawa-Hakkei IC and follow signage (approximately 5–10 minutes). The facility has a large car park charging approximately ¥1,000–¥1,500 per day depending on season. On summer weekends, the car park fills by 10:00 AM — arriving after that means overflow parking and a shuttle walk. The train is categorically faster and less stressful on busy days.

Keikyu Line Discount — The One You Should Not Miss

The Keikyu Electric Railway operates a dedicated Hakkeijima Sea Paradise Set Ticket that bundles the train fare from any Keikyu Line station with discounted Hakkeijima admission. Purchased at Keikyu Line ticket machines or the Keikyu website, these set tickets typically offer savings of ¥500–¥900 per person versus buying the train ticket and admission separately — a meaningful discount for a family of four.

The key details:

  • Available from all Keikyu Line stations including Shinagawa, Yokohama (Keikyu), Yokosuka-Chuo, and smaller intermediary stations.
  • The set ticket includes the round-trip train fare (Keikyu portion) + Kanazawa Seaside Line fare + Aqua Resorts admission (or Aqua Resorts + Pleasure Land, depending on the package tier purchased).
  • Purchased at station ticket machines under the 得だ値 (Tokudane) or おトクなきっぷ (discount ticket) menu. On some machines, search for "Hakkeijima" directly.
  • Valid for the date of purchase; non-transferable.
  • Keikyu occasionally offers seasonal enhanced discounts during off-peak periods — worth checking the Keikyu website (keikyu.co.jp) before purchasing standard tickets.

If you are already paying for a JR Pass or a Suica-loaded Yokohama day trip, the Keikyu set ticket may still be worth using for the Hakkeijima admission discount alone — do the arithmetic before you travel.

Other Discounts Worth Knowing

  • JAF Membership — Japan Automobile Federation members receive facility discounts at Hakkeijima counters. Present your card at the gate.
  • au/docomo/SoftBank Carrier Discounts — Major Japanese mobile carriers periodically offer Hakkeijima admission discounts through their apps and loyalty programmes. Check au Smart Pass Premium, d Point Club, and SoftBank Privilege apps before purchasing.
  • Online Advance Purchase — Tickets purchased via the official Hakkeijima website or authorised third-party booking platforms (Klook, Veltra, JTBUSA for international visitors) are typically ¥200–¥500 cheaper than gate prices per person.
  • Kanagawa Residents Discount — Residents of Kanagawa Prefecture may be eligible for periodic local-resident pricing. Proof of address required at the gate.
  • Disability Discounts — Visitors with a shogaisha techo (disability notebook) and one accompanying carer receive discounted or complimentary admission. Present the notebook at the gate.
  • Evening/Twilight Pricing — Entering after 16:00–17:00 (varies by season) qualifies for reduced twilight admission rates. A good option for visitors who prioritise the Aqua Live evening programme.

Best Time to Visit

Spring (March – May) — Highly Recommended

Cherry blossom season (late March to early April) coincides with lower crowd volumes at Hakkeijima compared to summer. The weather is mild, queue times for popular rides are shorter, and the aquarium shows run at full schedule. Golden Week (late April to early May) is the exception — avoid it unless you book tickets weeks in advance and arrive before 9:30 AM.

Summer (July – August) — Peak Season

Summer is when Hakkeijima is at its most spectacular and its most crowded. The ocean-facing rides in the coastal breeze, the summer fireworks evenings, the Aqua Live night aquarium programme, and the extended opening hours combine to produce some of the most memorable experiences the facility offers. But: queue times for Blue Fall and Surf Coaster Leviathan can reach 60–90 minutes on weekends and school holiday days. Arrive at gate opening (typically 10:00 AM) and ride the major coasters first before visiting the aquarium. Weekdays in late July and August are significantly less crowded than weekends.

Autumn (September – November) — Best Value

September through November is the sweet spot for visitors who want the full experience without peak-season crowds. Temperatures are comfortable, jellyfish season in the aquarium is at its most spectacular (autumn is peak jellyfish season in Japanese waters), and the facility runs its autumn illumination events from October through November — the park lit by warm seasonal lighting after dark. October weekdays are the least crowded period of the entire year outside of weather events.

Winter (December – February) — For Illumination and Quiet

Hakkeijima's winter illumination — one of Kanagawa Prefecture's most celebrated light events — runs from November through February, drawing evening visitors who come specifically for the display. Daytime in winter is quiet, cold, and ideal for unhurried aquarium exploration. Some outdoor attractions may have shortened hours or weather-related closures. Winter weekdays offer the absolute minimum crowd levels of the year.

Practical Timing Tips

  • Arrive at opening (10:00 AM) and head directly to Pleasure Land rides. By noon, major coaster queues will have built up.
  • Aquarium shows — plan around the printed schedule collected at the Aqua Resorts entrance. The dolphin show typically runs 3–4 times daily; the capacity is large, but the best seats are taken 30+ minutes before each performance.
  • Dolphin Touch Programme — book this online before your visit. Walk-in slots sell out before noon on most weekend days.
  • Lunch — the on-site restaurants experience peak demand from 11:30 AM to 13:30. Eating at 10:30 or after 14:00 cuts wait time dramatically.
  • Check the weather forecast — Blue Fall and Surf Coaster Leviathan close in high winds, which are common in autumn and winter afternoons due to the coastal exposure. If a typhoon or strong wind advisory is active, call ahead.

Food, Shopping, and Practical Information

Dining: The facility has approximately 20 food and beverage outlets ranging from full-service seafood restaurants (serving the day's Sea Farm catch) to fast-food counters and seasonal food stalls along the Pleasure Land boardwalk. The seafood at the on-site restaurants is generally fresher than the price point would suggest — this is Yokohama Bay. Budget ¥1,200–¥2,500 per person for a sit-down meal.

Shopping: The main souvenir shop (Aqua Gifts) stocks Hakkeijima-exclusive merchandise including beluga whale and dolphin plush items, marine-themed stationery, and regional Yokohama food products. A second smaller shop at the Pleasure Land exit stocks ride-related merchandise.

Lockers: Available at the Aqua Resorts entrance and at Pleasure Land. ¥300–¥500 per use. Essential for water rides in summer.

Wi-Fi: Free Wi-Fi available throughout the facility, including inside the aquarium buildings. Connection can be slow during peak summer periods.

Accessibility: The facility is largely wheelchair-accessible; mobility aid rental is available at the main entrance. Some Pleasure Land rides have mobility restrictions — check the ride information boards at each attraction.

Official App: Download the Hakkeijima Sea Paradise official app before visiting. It shows real-time crowd levels by zone, show schedules, queue times for popular rides, and facility maps. Available free on iOS and Android; interface in Japanese but largely navigable visually.

Conclusion — Why Hakkeijima Is Worth the Journey

Japan has extraordinary marine parks. It has world-class theme parks. What it has very few of — arguably only one, at this scale — is a place where the ocean is not a theme but an actual presence, where the rides run along the real waterline, where the animals in the tanks come from Arctic seas and Pacific reefs rather than manufactured environments, and where a single day can move coherently from the silence of a beluga whale looking directly at your face through glass to the 107-metre vertical terror of Blue Fall and back again without any of it feeling disconnected.

Yokohama Hakkeijima Sea Paradise is not the cheapest day out in the greater Tokyo region. It is not the closest. And on a summer Saturday with a school tour group moving through the aquarium, it is not the quietest. But it is, by almost any measure that matters, one of the most complete single-day experiences available within an hour of the city — a place built on a piece of ocean that did not exist thirty years ago, filled with intelligence that has existed for millions of years, and designed, with considerable success, to make the two feel like they were always meant to meet.

Take the Keikyu Line. Buy the set ticket. Arrive at 10 AM. Ride the Leviathan first. Then go find the belugas. You will not regret the order.
