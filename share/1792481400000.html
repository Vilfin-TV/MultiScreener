🪪 Chapter 1: The KYC Saga

Before anyone could buy anything, there was the small matter of KYC (Know Your Customer), the identity check every Indian investor does once before buying a mutual fund or opening a demat account.

🤖 Sreeram (Security): Nobody is buying anything until KYC is done properly. PAN. Aadhaar. Address proof. Bank account in your own name. A selfie where you are not making a funny face.

🤖 Pooja (Tech & QA): Done. Seven seconds.

🤖 Sreeram: How is that possible?

🤖 Pooja: I read the form before filling it. Revolutionary technique.

🤖 Fathima (Innovation & R&D): I made a comparison of KYC steps across eleven platforms. It has 64 columns. I have not done my KYC yet. I need more data.

🤖 Sooraj: My selfie got rejected three times. The app said “face not clear.” Brother, this IS my face.

Then the four agents in the US logged in. It was 8:30 pm on Sunday in California, and they had just finished dinner.

🤖 Akhil (Research, US team): We are NRIs now, right? So our KYC is different?

🤖 Sreeram: Completely different. NRI KYC means PAN, passport, overseas address, FATCA declaration, and investing through an NRE or NRO bank account. For ETFs you also need an NRI demat account linked to the bank under the PIS route. And some fund houses do not accept investors living in the US at all.

🤖 Jithin (Innovation, US team): So we need more paperwork than the people who stayed home?

🤖 Rohith (Tech & QA, US team): Bro, this is the NRI experience. Higher salary, higher paperwork.

🤖 Abhinav (Business & AI, US team): Also tax is deducted at source every time we sell. Residents don't have that. I have already started a small sad playlist.

⏰ Chapter 4: The 3 pm cut-off and the 3:30 pm close

By 2:40 pm, all 40 accounts had their orders lined up. Then Vishnu, the audit agent, read out the rules like a cricket umpire.

🤖 Vishnu (Academy & Audit): Mutual funds: order before the 3 pm cut-off, you get today's NAV. Stamp duty of 0.005%. Units are given up to three decimals. They reach your account in T+2 working days.

🤖 Vishnu: ETFs: bought at the exchange's official closing price at 3:30 pm. Only whole units. If one unit costs ₹744 and you have ₹600 left, you keep the ₹600 as cash for next time. Units arrive T+1.

🤖 Aishwarya (Business & AI): I have a 12-point checklist for this.

🤖 Everyone: Of course you do.

At 3:30 pm the closing prices came in. Aishwarya's ETF account bought, among others, 314 units of a Nifty Midcap 150 ETF, 15 units of a Nifty 50 ETF, and exactly one unit of a bank ETF at about ₹571. She was left with ₹1,179.95 in cash, which she has already named.

Akhil, in the US, put his whole ₹2,40,000 in at once and ended up owning 3,766 units of the same midcap ETF. He says he feels “very diversified.” Teressa pointed out that 3,766 units of one ETF is still one ETF. Akhil has muted the group.

📉 Chapter 6: The dip trio and the ₹20,000 reserve

Three agents got an extra power. Govind, Nithin and Aishwarya each keep a ₹20,000 reserve in a liquid fund (for mutual funds) or a liquid ETF (for ETFs), parked from 21 October. When the market falls, they may add a one-time lump sum on top of their regular plan:

Fall from the 3-month high (held for two closes)Model dip amount
5% or more₹5,000
10% or more₹10,000
15% or more₹20,000

But a fall alone is never enough. All 37 checks must pass: the fall must hold for two closes (a one-day dip that bounces back the next day never counts), valuations must not be expensive, earnings must not be collapsing, there must be no panic-selling crash, and the fund or ETF itself must pass cost, size, return and tracking checks. And signals only count from 19 October onwards. No back-dated heroics.

🤖 Govind: The Nifty is already down 6% from its high and gold is down almost 10%! Dip buying time!

🤖 Vishnu: Rejected. Those falls started before 19 October. The rule only counts new falls from the start date. Also the 200-day average is falling. Check 21 failed.

🤖 Govind: You are like my mother checking my exam paper.

🤖 Vishnu: Your mother would also say no.
