OTT Update: September's Best Picks Still Streaming + October Arrivals + What's Coming in November 2026

October 16, 2026

Updated October 16, 2026 — comprehensive, cross-checked guide covering last month's OTT highlights, October's live and upcoming drops, theatrical blockbusters heading to your screen, and the biggest streaming event of 2026 on the horizon.

Three weeks into October and the streaming landscape has rarely felt this charged. Drishyam The Conclusion just crossed ₹507 crore globally in only two weeks — Vijay Salgaonkar's franchise farewell is already the second highest-grossing Indian film of 2026, and the clock is ticking before Ajay Devgn's most beloved character arrives on your sofa. Jailer 2 released yesterday with Rajinikanth as Tiger Muthuvel Pandian, Vidya Balan in a fierce new avatar, and Hrithik Roshan in a cameo that has Reddit in chaos — that one will land on JioHotstar within six weeks. Meanwhile, Netflix's October OTT premiere is a Neeraj Pandey crime thriller built around Manoj Bajpayee playing a bent Delhi cop on the most chaotic night of his career, and it arrives in exactly one week. And hovering over all of it, just three weeks away in cinemas: Ramayana – Part: I — Ranbir Kapoor as Ram, Yash as Ravana, Sai Pallavi as Sita, Hans Zimmer and A.R. Rahman sharing a score, and Sunny Deol as Hanuman waiting at the end of the film like Baahubali's "Why Kattappa killed Baahubali?" moment. India's biggest theatrical gamble of the decade lands November 5. Here is everything — September's OTT slate you may have missed, October's confirmed drops, and the blockbuster calendar through the end of the year.

October's Big OTT Premiere — Gajab Raat (Netflix, Oct 23)

Gajab Raat — Netflix | Oct 23 | Hindi Crime Thriller

Director: Neeraj Pandey  |  Cast: Manoj Bajpayee, Divya Dutta, Nushrratt Bharuccha, Saqib Saleem, Akshay Oberoi, Kiku Sharda

Inspector Ajay Dikshit — a corrupt, money-hungry Delhi cop with plans for a very profitable night — has his scheme spectacularly derailed when a badly injured girl is thrown from a moving car in the heart of the city. What begins as one inconvenient case spirals into a global conspiracy that Dikshit never wanted and cannot walk away from. Neeraj Pandey (Special 26, Baby, A Wednesday) returns to Netflix after the film's original title controversy forced a rename. Manoj Bajpayee plays his most morally ambiguous role in years — not the noble cop but the pragmatic one suddenly in far over his head. Trailer reaction has been overwhelmingly positive.

October 23 OTT Drop — Sumo Didi (ZEE5)

Sumo Didi — ZEE5 | Oct 23 | Hindi Sports Drama

Director: Jayant Rohatgi  |  Cast: Shriyam Bhagnani, Anubha Fatehpura, Nitesh Pandey, Chaitanya Sharma

Inspired by the true story of Hetal Dave — India's pioneering female sumo wrestler who broke every barrier the sport could place in her path. A sports drama with a completely uncharted subject for Indian cinema, and that novelty alone makes it worth a watch when it lands on October 23.

September OTT — The Headlines Worth Catching Up On

NETFLIX — September Picks Still Streaming

  • Shaque: Trust No One (Hindi, 8 eps) — Parineeti Chopra plays a mother who, nine years after her newborn was abducted in a hospital, receives a lead so implausible it might destroy her marriage if she follows it. A slow-burn thriller that earns every twist. The best Hindi series premiere of September.
  • UNABOMBER (English) — Russell Crowe as the Harvard psychologist whose academic research into personal transformation may have inadvertently shaped the worldview of Ted Kaczynski. Jacob Tremblay as young Kaczynski, Shailene Woodley as the journalist who cracked the case. Gripping and uncomfortable.
  • Baby Do Die Do / Great Indian Kapil Show S5 — lighter options for when you want entertainment without the homework.

PRIME VIDEO — September Picks Still Streaming

  • Awarapan 2 (Hindi, 142 min) — Emraan Hashmi returns to the role that defined a generation. The original Awarapan (2007) was cult. The sequel carries the weight of that legacy with mixed but nostalgic audience response.
  • Don't Be Shy (Hindi) — Lands directly on Prime. Recommended for an uncomplicated Friday evening.
  • Ramba Oorvasi Menaka (Telugu + dubs) — Three celestial nymphs descend after 25 years to fulfil a childhood promise. Allari Naresh + Vennela Kishore comedy pairing guarantees genuine laughs.
  • The Love Hypothesis (English) — Based on Ali Hazel's bestselling novel. A PhD student fake-dates her professor to keep her ex at bay; warm romantic chaos follows.

JIOHOTSTAR — September Picks Still Streaming

  • Hunkkaar: The Roar (Hindi, 8 eps) — A girl files an assault allegation against a powerful, politically connected cult leader. The series treats this with a ferocity that made it the most-discussed Indian OTT launch of September.
  • Mango Pachcha (Kannada + dubs, 95 min) — Mysuru-set crime drama. A CD shop owner discovers, after his father's death, a hidden half-brother and an inherited marijuana business. Lean and propulsive at 95 minutes.

ZEE5 — September Picks Still Streaming

  • Toxic: A Fairy Tale for Grown-Ups (Kannada + dubs, 192 min) — Yash, Kiara Advani, Nayanthara; Dir: Geetu Mohandas. The Rs.500-crore gangster epic that underperformed theatrically is now yours to judge at home. Critics were divided. The ambition is real. At three hours twelve minutes it demands patience — but Yash's final act is unlike anything he has delivered before.
  • Agadha (Telugu + dubs, 137 min) — Kamakshi Bhaskarla investigates her cousin's suspicious death and discovers a forbidden forest cave and an ancient dark entity. Atmospheric supernatural horror with genuine tension in the first half.

SONYLIV + APPLE TV+ — September Picks

  • Habeebi (SonyLIV, Tamil + dubs) — A 1980s period romance set in Tamil Nadu's handloom communities. Exquisitely crafted and likely to resonate strongly with Kerala audiences given the cultural proximity.
  • Brothers (Apple TV+, English, 8 eps) — Matthew McConaughey and Woody Harrelson play themselves as two men who discover through a DNA test they might literally be biological brothers. Concept: irresistibly clever. Execution: mixed 57% RT. The chemistry alone makes it worth checking out.

Jailer 2 — Full Story

Jailer 2 — Tamil + Hindi | Dir: Nelson Dilipkumar | Sun Pictures | Released Oct 15

Full Cast: Rajinikanth, Ramya Krishnan, Vidya Balan, SJ Suryah, Shiva Rajkumar, Mohanlal, Jackie Shroff, Suraj Venjaramoodu, Hrithik Roshan (special appearance) | Music: Anirudh Ravichander

After Tiger Muthuvel Pandian eliminates the Varman syndicate in Part 1, he relocates his grieving family to remote Tamil Nadu and lives as a quiet supermarket supervisor — nobody knows his past. But Varman's close aide Dileep (Suraj Venjaramoodu) is hunting for Varman's killer and three truckloads of hidden ancient statues. A mundane clash with a local fruit shop owner inadvertently leads Dileep's men straight to Tiger. Nelson Dilipkumar goes bigger in every dimension — the film ends on Hrithik Roshan's entry that reportedly has a Chulbul Pandey connection, explicitly promising Jailer 3. This is Rajinikanth at 75 doing things that will make you forget about age entirely.

Rated U/A 16+ | Advance booking: 14.15 lakh tickets on Day 1 alone | Vidya Balan in fierce villain mode

Drishyam The Conclusion — Full Story

Drishyam The Conclusion — Hindi | Oct 2 | Dir: Abhishek Pathak

Cast: Ajay Devgn, Shriya Saran, Ishita Dutta, Tabu

Years after covering up the accidental death that launched the entire franchise, Vijay Salgaonkar's family faces a new adversary — someone close enough to the original truth to destroy everything Vijay has protected. Using the same cinematic ingenuity and borrowed movie logic that have defined the character across three films, Vijay devises elaborate plans while confronting betrayal and sacrifice. Mohanlal, who played Vijay in the original Malayalam Drishyam, publicly praised the conclusion. Makers have confirmed there will be no Drishyam 4 — a rare Indian franchise that ends by deliberate choice.

Rs.507 crore worldwide | Second highest-grossing Indian film of 2026 | Rs.240 crore opening week

Prahaar — The Ujjwal Nikam Story — Full Story

Prahaar — Hindi | Oct 16 | Dir: Avinash Arun

Cast: Rajkummar Rao as Ujjwal Nikam, Lalit Prabhakar as Ajmal Kasab, Ritvik Sahore in a pivotal role

A courtroom legal drama built on the career of Ujjwal Nikam — the Special Public Prosecutor who secured the death sentence against Ajmal Kasab for the 26/11 Mumbai attacks. Rajkummar Rao delivers what early critics are calling the performance of his career. Taran Adarsh calls it a "must watch experience." Director Avinash Arun brings a rigorous visual discipline to the courtroom that makes procedural drama feel like a thriller. Released today — early audience reaction is very strong.

The Biggest Event of 2026 — Ramayana Part I (Nov 5 Theatres, OTT Jan–Feb 2027)

India's Most Anticipated Film of the Decade — In Cinemas November 5, 2026

Ramayana – Part: I

Director: Nitesh Tiwari (Dangal, Chhichhore) | Producer: Namit Malhotra (Prime Focus) | Music: Hans Zimmer + A.R. Rahman — first-ever collaboration on an Indian film

Complete Cast

  • Lord Ram: Ranbir Kapoor
  • Ravana: Yash (KGF fame)
  • Sita: Sai Pallavi
  • Surpanakha: Rakul Preet Singh
  • Queen Kaikeyi: Lara Dutta
  • King Dashrath: Arun Govil — the original TV Ram returns as Ram's own father
  • Manthara: Sheeba Chadha
  • Jatayu (voice): Anupam Kher
  • Hanuman: Sunny Deol — revealed at film's end, Baahubali-style

The Scale

  • Two-part epic — Part II arrives October 2027
  • Part II already 50% completed in shooting
  • International trailer unveiled Bengaluru, Oct 16
  • Hans Zimmer + AR Rahman — unprecedented
  • Baahubali-level anticipation for Hanuman reveal
  • OTT window estimate: Jan–Feb 2027

The casting of Arun Govil — who played Lord Ram in the original Doordarshan Ramayana that an entire generation grew up watching — as King Dashrath is a masterstroke of narrative poetry. The man who was Ram for India now plays Ram's father, passing the mantle on screen. Whether Nitesh Tiwari's Ramayana soars or stumbles, nothing else in Indian cinema this year — or this decade — will be as widely discussed.
