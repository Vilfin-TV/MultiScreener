🎬 Malayalam OTT Spotlight

Malayalam cinema is on a 2026 peak. Kishkindha Kaandam (Netflix) stands as the year's most discussed thriller — a neighbourhood paranoia drama with zero action and maximum tension, written and directed with surgical precision. Antappante Athbudha Pravarthikal (Prime Video, IMDb 8.6) is possibly the finest Malayalam film of the past three years. This week, Pradhama Drishtiya Kuttakkar (Netflix) has opened to strong critical response — early word describes it as a psychological procedural that turns the crime-thriller format inside out. And Khalifa (Prime), Prithviraj's Oct 17 drop, delivers exactly what the trailer promised: lean, brutal, no padding.

🎭 Hindi Blockbusters on OTT

Hunkkaar: The Roar (JioHotstar, 8.2) is the Hindi OTT event of September — a courtroom drama about a whistleblower attorney that draws comparisons to Jolly LLB but with darker, longer reach. Shaque: Trust No One (Netflix, 8.0) is the psychological thriller you've been waiting for since Andhadhun. And if you missed Stree 3 in cinemas, it's now on Prime Video — Rajkummar Rao's horror-comedy universe delivering on every promise of the franchise. This week: Bhool Bhulaiyaa 4 (Netflix, Oct 18) with Kartik Aaryan continuing the franchise. Gajab Raat (Netflix, Oct 23) is the most anticipated Hindi OTT drop of the month — Neeraj Pandey directing Manoj Bajpayee as a corrupt Delhi cop in moral freefall over a single catastrophic night.

🌟 Tamil & Telugu — What's Worth It

Amaran (Netflix, 8.5) is the Tamil sleeper hit of 2026 — Sivakarthikeyan in a biographical war drama that demolished expectations and is now one of the most re-watched films on the platform. Retro (Netflix, Oct 19) — Suriya and Karthik Subbaraj reuniting after Jigarthanda DoubleX — is being treated as the most important Tamil release of Q4. In Telugu, Saripodhaa Sanivaaram (Prime, 7.8) holds up as a stylish Saturday-night watch, and Maharaja (Netflix, Oct 21) brings Vijay Sethupathi back in a tightly plotted thriller.

🎬 Ramayana – Part I: Why This Matters

Hans Zimmer and A.R. Rahman co-scoring a single film has never happened before. Ranbir Kapoor as Ram, Yash as Ravana, Sai Pallavi as Sita, Sunny Deol as Hanuman — and the film ends where Baahubali trained a whole generation to expect the biggest cliffhanger: the moment Sita is taken. Part II isn't dated yet. India's ₹800+ crore opening weekend is already predicted. It opens Nov 5 — nineteen days from now.
