India's OTT Roundup: Every Major Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam & English Release This Month

July 19, 2026

Updated July 19, 2026 — expanded with additional confirmed releases and poster art for every title.

Grab the remote and hold it steady, because this month five entire film industries have been throwing their best (and their weirdest) straight onto your streaming home screen at once. Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam and Hollywood all had a moment in June and July 2026 — a reunion twenty years in the making, a Michael Jackson biopic critics loved to hate and audiences couldn't stop watching, a spy thriller that got savaged by critics and still pulled a claimed 50 million viewers in one weekend, and at least one film about a film industry roasting itself. Here's the full, cross-checked rundown of what actually landed on Netflix, Prime Video, JioHotstar and the rest — with a poster for every title so you know exactly what you're looking for — and what's still coming before July runs out.

Hindi: A Dark Comedy, a Border Reunion, a Rom-Com That Split the Room — and a Streaming Juggernaut Critics Hated

Bollywood's OTT month opened strong and got messier as it went along. The clear critical winner was Maa Behen, a black comedy that turned the everyday cruelty of small-town gossip into something genuinely biting — Madhuri Dixit and Triptii Dimri anchoring what more than one reviewer called a sharp takedown of how society polices women's lives. Ikka reunited Sunny Deol and Akshaye Khanna for the first time since 1997's Border — a genuine "wait, THOSE two are back together?" moment. But the real story is Dhurandhar: The Revenge: Rotten Tomatoes gave it a rough 33%, with some international critics calling it outright propaganda — and JioHotstar reported 50 million viewers in its opening weekend anyway, making it, by the platform's own count, the biggest digital launch in its history. Critics and crowds were watching two completely different movies.

Tamil: Action, an Unlikely Fantasy, and a Family Drama That Overstayed Its Welcome

Tamil cinema's OTT slate leaned toward heart over spectacle. Double Occupancy — a genuinely odd-sounding body-swap fantasy about a child who is a girl by day and a boy by night — turned out to be one of the month's pleasant surprises, praised for clever world-building from newcomer director Aswin Kandasamy. Blast brought the muscle, with Arjun Sarja headlining an action thriller that reviewers said had "big bang ideas" that occasionally fizzled before the final reel. On the comedy front, Parimala and Co reunited Jayaram and Urvashi for their first full pairing since 2002's Panchatanthiram — a reunion with real nostalgia value that critics almost universally felt the film squandered, with the Indian Express calling it "a lazy disaster."

Telugu: A Sports Drama Controversy, a Mystery That Lost the Plot, and Three More Nobody Saw Coming

Telugu's biggest OTT arrival by far was Peddi, Ram Charan and Janhvi Kapoor's sports-action drama that had already crossed ₹339 crore theatrically before landing on Netflix — though the digital release reignited backlash over scenes critics called objectifying toward Kapoor, prompting an apology from the makers. On the smaller end, Mareechika had all the ingredients of a gripping mystery thriller and, according to most reviewers, misplaced several of them somewhere in the second act. Three more original Telugu titles rounded out a busier month than it first looked: Ugly Story tackled marital abuse head-on and earned its leads career-best reviews even where critics found the film itself uneven, while Razor and Transfer Trimurthulu both leaned on familiar genre formulas that reviewers felt had aged out of style.

Malayalam: The Reunion Nobody Thought They'd See, a Trilogy's Final Bow, and the Busiest Slate of the Month

If one Malayalam release defined the month, it was Patriot — the first film to pair Mammootty and Mohanlal together in eighteen years. Two icons who spent two decades as separate universes, sharing a spy thriller about data privacy and national loyalty. Reviewers called it a slow burn that "tests your patience" in places but is anchored by Mammootty at his most grounded. Meanwhile, Drishyam 3 quietly closed out a trilogy that started all the way back in 2013, sending Georgekutty's story off to a mostly warm (if divisive) reception. And in the film that most deserves the word "comedy" in this whole roundup, Mollywood Times turned the camera on Malayalam cinema itself — a dark comedy about the industry that one critic called "one of the sharpest love-hate letters written to Malayalam cinema." Beyond the headliners, Malayalam quietly had the fullest release calendar of any industry this month — six more titles across horror-comedy, crime and sports genres, several riding on Shane Nigam and Sreenath Bhasi appearing in multiple films apiece.

English: Critics vs. Audiences Go to War Over Michael Jackson

The English-language slate delivered the month's single most entertaining discriminator: Michael, the Michael Jackson biopic starring his own nephew Jaafar Jackson, split critics and audiences almost exactly in half. Critics gave it a rough 38% — calling it "a greatest hits album" light on real insight — while audiences handed it a near-unanimous 97% approval. Same film, two completely different verdicts, depending who's asked. Elsewhere, Enola Holmes 3 brought Millie Bobby Brown and Henry Cavill back for a darker, more sophisticated outing under a new director, Ready or Not 2: Here I Come gave Samara Weaving's in-laws-from-hell franchise a genuinely well-reviewed sequel, and Wuthering Heights — Emerald Fennell's take on the Bronte classic, with Jacob Elordi and Margot Robbie — landed as genuinely divisive rather than a flat flop, whatever India's underwhelming box office numbers might suggest on their own.

Don't Mix These Up: The Same Story, Four Different Languages

One genuinely confusing pattern this month: a single film often streams under several different names and casts depending on language. Drishyam 3 is a Malayalam original (Mohanlal, directed by Jeethu Joseph) that was simultaneously dubbed into Tamil, Telugu and Kannada — so if you spot a "Telugu Drishyam 3" trending, that's the same film, not a remake. Similarly, Veerabhadrudu, which shows up on Telugu OTT charts with Suriya and Trisha Krishnan, is actually the Tamil film Karuppu dubbed over — a different production entirely from the Telugu-original titles in this list. Worth knowing before you tell a friend "you have to watch the Telugu one" when you actually watched the Malayalam cut.

The Big Picture

Line all five industries up side by side and a pattern emerges: this was a month of reunions and risk-taking rather than safe sequels, with a running argument between critics and everybody else. Mammootty and Mohanlal shared a screen for the first time in eighteen years. Sunny Deol and Akshaye Khanna did the same after almost three decades. Jayaram and Urvashi reunited after 2002 — even if the film around them didn't land. And twice this month, the review scores and the actual audience numbers told completely different stories: Michael's critics-vs-fans split, and Dhurandhar's savaged reviews next to a claimed 50 million streams in one weekend. If there's one thing to actually watch this week, split the difference: something loud (Peddi or Dhurandhar), something quiet (29 or Ugly Story), and one absolute must-not-miss reunion (Patriot). Your watchlist writes itself.
