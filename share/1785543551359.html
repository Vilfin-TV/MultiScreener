🏆 India Multi Cap Fund Comparison 2026: Kotak vs Nippon vs HSBC vs Axis vs SBI vs Mahindra vs Bajaj — Complete Analysis

August 1, 2026

📊 SEBI Multi Cap Category Deep Dive — July 2026 — All return data sourced from AMFI official NAV database (mfapi.in). Risk metrics computed over 3-year rolling windows. AUM as of June 2026. This is an educational comparative analysis — not investment advice.

The SEBI Multi Cap category is one of India's most democratically diversified equity mandates. Unlike Flexi Cap funds that allow managers to concentrate in Large Caps, Multi Cap funds are mandated to hold minimum 25% each in Large Cap, Mid Cap, and Small Cap stocks — ensuring genuine market-cap diversity in every portfolio. With 7 prominent funds now competing for this mandate, we put Kotak, Nippon, HSBC, Axis, SBI, Mahindra Manulife, and Bajaj Finserv under the microscope.

🏆 Conclusion — Which Multi Cap Fund to Choose?

🥇 Best Overall: Axis Multicap Fund — Highest 1Y return (9.7%), best Sharpe ratio (1.31), strongest alpha (5.5%) and reasonable AUM. Quality-first stock selection with a mid-cap tilt makes it the standout in the current market cycle. Best suited for investors with 5+ year horizon.
🥈 Best Risk-Adjusted: Kotak Multicap Fund — Lowest expense ratio (0.48%), consistent 3Y outperformance (21.0%), balanced allocation. The cost advantage compounds significantly over time. Ideal for cost-conscious long-term investors.
🥉 Best Track Record: Nippon India Multi Cap Fund — 13 years of history, ₹19,247 Cr AUM, 5Y CAGR of 20.9%. The only fund with a true long-term track record. Best for risk-averse investors who want battle-tested performance.
🌟 Hidden Gem: Mahindra Manulife Multi Cap Fund — 9-year track record (oldest after Nippon), 5Y CAGR 18.8%, mid-small tilt with experienced managers. Under-researched and under-owned — worth serious consideration.
⚠️ Avoid for Now: SBI Multicap Fund — Lowest 1Y (0.8%) and 3Y (15.0%) returns, negative alpha (-2.8%), same expense ratio as Nippon (0.75%). Despite brand trust, the performance data does not justify inclusion over better options in the same category.
