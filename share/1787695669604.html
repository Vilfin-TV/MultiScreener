PowerUp Money Elite Review: The ₹999/Year SEBI-Registered Fund Advisor Trusted by 11 Lakh Investors

August 26, 2026

August 2026. An IT professional in Bengaluru opens his mutual fund portfolio. The SIP he started five years ago has grown to over ₹9 lakh — a solid start. But the mid-cap fund that carried a top rating back in 2021 now sits at rank 52 within its own category. He doesn't know this. That gap — between the assumption that a fund is "still doing well" and its actual peer-relative performance — is one of the most common and costly blind spots in Indian retail investing. Filling that gap is exactly what Bengaluru-based startup PowerUp Money set out to do in 2024.

What Is PowerUp Money?

PowerUp Money (Uniorbit Wealth Management Pvt Ltd) is a SEBI-Registered Investment Advisor — registration number INA000019798. The app imports a user's existing mutual fund portfolio via PAN in read-only mode, then assesses the health of each fund: which are performing well relative to peers, which are underperforming, and which deserve an exit. The recommendations are data-driven, commission-free, and built on over 20 years of historical fund data.

The company was founded by Prateek Jindal — an IIM Mumbai alumnus with 11+ years of fintech experience, including a stint at credit card fintech Uni Cards. Since its 2024 launch, PowerUp Money has raised $19.1 million (approximately ₹160 crore) across seed and Series A rounds, backed by Peak XV Partners (formerly Sequoia India), Accel, Blume Ventures, and Kae Capital. By mid-2026 the platform has surpassed 11 lakh users, tracks over ₹1,80,000 crore in AUM, and holds a 4.9/5 app rating across more than 9,000 reviews.

Three Terms You Need to Understand First

Before diving into what Elite unlocks, three core concepts define how PowerUp Money works:

Power Rank — A fund's rank within its own SEBI category, calculated using 20+ years of historical data. If a large-cap fund sits at rank 38 in the Nifty 100 category, it means 37 funds in that category have scored better. This is category-relative — not a universal score.

Elite Score (CRV) — A composite score built from three sub-scores: Consistency (how steadily a fund outperforms its category average across rolling periods), Recency (recent momentum — is the fund currently in form or declining?), and Volatility (how well the fund protects capital during market drawdowns relative to peers). Together, these produce an Elite Score that goes meaningfully deeper than trailing-return-only star ratings.

Power Select — A side-by-side fund comparison tool for any category, showing Power Rank, Elite Score, AUM trend, and quartile color-coding in a single table. Elite members also get an "in-form funds only" filter to cut straight to the top performers.

The CRV Methodology: Why It's Different from Star Ratings

Most fund rating agencies — CRISIL, Value Research, Morningstar — lean heavily on trailing returns over standardised periods (1Y, 3Y, 5Y). PowerUp Money's CRV framework is built differently, using 20+ years of data across three dimensions:

Consistency measures how frequently a fund outperforms its category average across multiple rolling periods. A fund that delivered exceptional returns one year and then sharp losses the next scores poorly on Consistency — even if its 3-year CAGR looks decent in aggregate.

Recency captures current momentum. It answers the question: is this fund in form right now, or has it started to drift? A fund with a strong long-term track record but deteriorating recent performance will show this in its Recency score before it shows up in trailing returns.

Volatility measures downside protection relative to category peers — how much of a portfolio's value a fund preserves during market corrections, compared to others in the same category.

The precise mathematical weightings between these three factors are proprietary and not publicly disclosed — independent verification of the exact formula is not possible. The framework structure, however, is confirmed across official blog posts and product documentation.

Three Things Not to Confuse About PowerUp Money Elite

  1. PowerUp Money is not a trading platform. It is a research and advisory tool. To actually invest, you still need a separate platform — Zerodha Coin, Kuvera, Groww, Navi, or similar. The direct MF execution feature is not yet live.
  2. Power Rank is not the same as CRISIL/Value Research star ratings. The CRV methodology produces different results from traditional rating frameworks — a 5-star fund on Value Research may rank mid-table on Power Rank, and vice versa. Neither is definitively correct; they measure different things.
  3. Elite is not a 1:1 personal financial advisor. PowerUp Money gives data-driven, category-relative signals. It does not know your tax bracket, income stability, risk capacity, or life goals. For personalised financial planning, a licensed advisor is still the right call.

Do You Actually Need Elite?

Before subscribing, ask yourself this honestly: investors with a mutual fund portfolio under ₹2 lakh will likely find the free tier's basic health check adequate at this stage. But for anyone with a portfolio above ₹5 lakh, identifying even one underperforming fund — switching it from a regular to a direct plan, or replacing it with a higher-ranked alternative — can recover the ₹999 subscription cost many times over in a single year.

The Indian mutual fund market currently has over 1,500 active schemes. SEBI data has repeatedly confirmed that top-quartile performers in one period frequently slip to the bottom quartile three years later. The problem isn't choosing a bad fund at entry — it's not noticing when a good fund turns bad. PowerUp Money Elite is, in that sense, a portfolio X-ray machine: it surfaces problems that trailing return charts obscure. The actual investing — buying, selling, switching — still happens on your broker or fund platform. Think of it as a diagnostic tool, not a replacement for one.

At ₹999 a year — roughly the cost of a mid-range restaurant dinner — the barrier to trying Elite is low. A 7-day free trial is available for one fund category before committing.
